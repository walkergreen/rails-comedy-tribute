About

In loving
memory

Rails Comedy was DC's improv and sketch comedy theater — a black box on the second floor of the DC Arts Center in Adams Morgan, where the shows were unscripted, the sketches were rehearsed within an inch of their lives, and the audience was always close enough to be part of the bit.

For five years it ran a full slate of house shows, indie team showcases, festivals, classes and workshops. Improvised Shakespeare on one night, a live blind date on another, three teams brawling for the audience's vote on a third. Newcomers got stage time. Experienced groups got a home. Everybody got a room that was genuinely rooting for them.

The theater has closed. This page is a tribute — the shows, the photographs, and the room they happened in, kept somewhere they won't disappear.

What played here

Pick your play

01

Improv

Entirely unscripted. Performers built the whole show on the spot, from a single suggestion, with nothing written down and no way to take it back.

02

Sketch

Meticulously crafted scenes where every punchline was written, cut, rewritten and rehearsed until it landed exactly where it was supposed to.

03

Improv and sketch classes and workshops for students of every level — including people who had never set foot on a stage before.

Festivals

DC Sketchfest and other showcases brought teams from across the city and beyond onto the Rails stage for long weekends of comedy.

The repertory

Past
shows

A selection of the shows that ran at Rails.

Improv

The Merely Players

Shakespeare, made up on the spot. The company improvised a full play in the bard's language, complete with his tropes, characters and settings — none of it written, all of it in iambic-ish pentameter.

Improv

Off Cue

Short form, played fast. Quick-fire games, sharp wit and total chaos, with scenes short enough that the wheels came off before anyone could fix them.

Improv · Competition

Cage Match

A monthly showdown. Three teams, one stage, no holds barred — and the audience decided who advanced. One team walked out Cage Match Champion. The other two walked out.

Sketch

Etch A Sketch

DC's premiere showcase of sketch comedy teams and players. A fast-paced night of scripted comedy, open to performers of every skill level.

Improv · Audience participation

Meet Cute

A live blind date show. Audience members signed up — or got signed up by a friend — to go on a real first date, on stage, in front of everyone. It went well more often than you'd think.

Sketch · Long form

Fanfiction Theater

Required reading, performed live and taken somewhere its authors never intended. Books got the book report they deserved, staged with total commitment and no respect whatsoever.

Sketch

Mother's Pouch

A lively night of sketch comedy — big laughs, unhinged performances and chaotic fun from one of the groups that made the Rails stage their own. Frequently sold out.

Sketch · Open mic

The Sketch Jam

Half open mic, half writers' group. Writers brought something new and watched it performed live for the first time. Newcomers welcome, signups in the first ten minutes, show immediately after.

Sketch · Awards

The Bobbys

DC's sketch comedy awards night — a lectern, sealed envelopes, unwise formalwear, and a room full of people who write comedy for nothing finally getting handed a trophy for it. The third annual edition ran at Rails in March 2026.

Guest · Sketch

Kevin McDonald

The Kids in the Hall founding member played Rails in June 2025 — one of the biggest names to work the room, in a theater where the front row was close enough to grab the mic.

Improv · Long form

After Hours

An improvised late-night talk show. Monologue, guests and segments invented on the spot, played completely straight right up until the format collapsed.

Improv · Ensemble

Happy Family Dinner

A recurring ensemble improv show and one of the fixtures of the Rails calendar, sharing a Tuesday night bill with After Hours through the autumn of 2025.

Solo

Danielle Steger Finds a Job!

A one-woman show about the indignities of the job hunt, performed with a laptop and a slowly collapsing sense of optimism. Shared a bill with The RENS in October 2025.

Festival

DC Sketchfest

The annual festival that filled the room with sketch teams from across the city and beyond — several days of back-to-back comedy, and the busiest the theater ever got.

The stage

2438 18th St NW

Rails Comedy lived at the DC Arts Center in Adams Morgan — a small black box theater up a narrow staircase, with a low ceiling, folding seats and a lighting board that had opinions. It sat close enough to the stage that the front row was, functionally, part of the cast.

DCAC is still there, still an arts center, still worth your money. If you want to keep a room like this one going, go see something in it.

After 5 years and 500 shows, Rails Comedy's final season is here. Whether you've been with us since day 1 or attended your first Rails show last weekend, thank you for letting us be a part of the DC community.
